## New chemical reaction and scientific limitations

O2+ + electron -> 2 neutral O-equivalent atoms.

Peverall et al., J. Chem. Phys. **114**, 6679-6689 (2001),
https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1349079:
  k = 2.4e-13 (300/T_e[K])^0.70 [m3/s], where T_e[K] = 11604.518...
  * (2/3) * mean_electron_energy[eV].

This source characterizes predominantly vibrational-ground O2+; at electron
temperatures typical of an ICP the rate-law usage may **extrapolate outside its
established thermal regime**. Hence this is an exploratory sensitivity study,
not a source-proven high-temperature cross-section benchmark. No rate floors
or silently clamped Te ranges are introduced.

The existing fluid input resolves only one O species. Real DR branches include
O(3P), O(1D), and O(1S). Therefore the ON overlay assigns two
**O-equivalent atoms** to the existing O state as a *coarse-grained all-O*
sensitivity convention; it must NOT be interpreted as a validated O(3P)
branching yield. Source-correct resolved electronic-state chemistry would
require separate, sourced branching and species/energy models.

The experiment additionally uses -mean_en*R as a *removed-electron energy*
proxy. It is not a validated energy-resolved DR electron loss operator.

## Conservation ownership

Define R [mol/(m3 s)] = k [m3/s] * ne [1/m3] * c_O2p [mol/m3].
Here ne = n_e_physical and c_O2p = exp(log_ni). One R owns all source terms:
electron -R, O2+ -R, O mass +0.032 R [kg/(m3 s)],
neutral mixture mass +0.032 R, charged heavy mass -0.032 R,
electron energy -mean_en*R [eV mol/(m3 s)].

By construction:
- oxygen atoms: 2 O gained per O2+ lost;
- heavy mass: +0.032R -0.032R = 0;
- charged species and electron: S_O2p - S_e = 0;
- no separate duplicated reaction progress owner.

The original model's O2 constraint and all other reactions remain intact.
